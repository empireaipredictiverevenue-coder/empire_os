## Independently looked-up official sources

1. [Brownfield land — Planning Data](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/dataset/brownfield-land)
   explicitly displays **Open Government Licence v.3.0** and attribution
   **© Crown copyright and database right 2026**.
2. [National Archives — Open Government Licence v3](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/)
   permits commercial and noncommercial reuse, copying, adaptation and
   distribution **of information covered by the licence**, with source
   acknowledgement and other conditions. It excludes personal data and
   unlicensed third-party rights, and disallows implied government endorsement.
3. [National Archives — OGL usage guidance](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/information-management/re-using-public-sector-information/uk-government-licensing-framework/open-government-licence/)
   says **no separate registration or application** is needed for use
   covered by OGL; it confirms attribution and personal-data limitations.

Source pages were publicly checked as background evidence; the exact live
publisher metadata may change. These are **research references**, not a
government-issued certification or legal sign-off. No content is copied
automatically from web search into a production DB.

## Provenance/freshness caution

`source_observed_at` is Empire's HTTP observation timestamp. It is
**not** a government-published source-update timestamp, a planning-decision
date, a construction start or a verified new lead. The Planning Data
dataset's displayed collection dates can lag or vary over time.

A second identical fetched snapshot 24 hours later proves only that
**the selected source fields have not changed between observations**.
It does not prove that government records were newly updated, nor that
we captured all events in the wider register.

No service, timer, new database, source capture, customer feed, or
outbound authority accompanies this rights/attribution package.
