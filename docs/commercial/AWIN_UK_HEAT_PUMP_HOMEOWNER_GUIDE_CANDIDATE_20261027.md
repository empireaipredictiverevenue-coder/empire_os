## H1: Is a heat pump right for your home? What to check before you request quotes

A heat pump transfers warmth from outdoors into your home using electricity. It can work in many UK properties, but the right answer is not determined by your floor area or postcode alone. A home with high heat loss, undersized radiators, limited outdoor space or poorly designed controls may need extra work for a heat pump to run effectively.

Start by checking your property and current heating system before looking at offers. A useful first step is the UK government's **Check if a heat pump could be suitable for you** service: https://www.gov.uk/check-heat-pump .

### 1. Check heat loss before comparing prices

Ask for a room-by-room heat-loss calculation—not merely a boiler-to-heat-pump replacement estimate. The calculation helps a competent installer decide what output is needed in colder weather, and whether individual radiators, pipework, or insulation need improvement.

A complete quotation should tell you **what the installer has assumed** about indoor temperatures, insulation, external design temperature and hot-water demand. If an offer has not assessed the building, ask what survey will happen before a final price or installation commitment.

### 2. Know which practical changes might be required

An air source heat pump generally needs an outdoor unit with suitable airflow and position. You may also need space for a hot-water cylinder, depending on the system. Older radiators can sometimes stay, but some homes benefit from larger radiators or changes to pipework and controls.

Questions worth asking:
- Can the installer show a **room-by-room heat-loss assessment** and explain the proposed system size?
- Will existing radiators heat each room at the expected operating temperature?
- Where will the outdoor unit sit, and what are the noise and neighbour considerations?
- Does the quote cover any cylinder, controls, radiators, electrical work and making-good?
- Which permissions or checks apply to a listed building, flat, leasehold home or conservation area?
- Who handles maintenance, warranty call-outs, commissioning and any snagging?

See GOV.UK's independent consumer guide: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/buying-green-heating-and-insulation-products-consumer-guide/buying-green-heating-and-insulation-products-a-consumer-guide .

### 3. Understand the Boiler Upgrade Scheme before you sign

**England and Wales:** As checked on 27 October 2026, qualifying installations can be eligible for **£7,500** towards an air-to-water air source heat pump or ground source heat pump. The scheme also includes **£2,500** for qualifying air-to-air heat pumps under its conditions.

A temporarily higher **£9,000** heat-pump grant is available for certain eligible off-gas-grid properties replacing oil or LPG heating, under rules applying until **31 March 2027**. Do not assume every off-grid property qualifies. Other property and installation restrictions apply, and the grant is administered through an **MCS-certified installer**. The installer applies on the property owner's behalf.

The scheme applies to England and Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland have different support routes. Grant figures and eligibility can change, so check the current official pages before relying on them.

Official sources:
- https://www.gov.uk/apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme/what-you-can-get
- https://www.gov.uk/apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme/check-if-youre-eligible
- https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/boiler-upgrade-scheme-bus/property-owners

### 4. Compare the *whole quote*, not only an advertised starting price

Ask each installer to show the equipment, survey, installation labour, electrical and plumbing changes, disposal, controls, VAT treatment, grant assumption, warranties and any chargeable exclusions. Find out when a survey becomes binding and what happens if the final design requires extra work.

Running costs depend on the home's heat demand, electricity tariff, system efficiency and how it is operated. There is **no universal guaranteed bill saving** from fitting a heat pump; a comparison requires the actual property details and fuel costs.

If a quotation promises a particular saving or payback period, request its assumptions in writing. Two proposals can use different heat-loss figures, emitter temperatures, hot-water assumptions or fuel-price forecasts and therefore lead to very different estimates.

### 5. Three signs you need more information

- An installer gives a final system size without investigating room-by-room heat loss.
- The quotation does not explain exclusions, radiator/cylinder changes or available grant conditions.
- You are pressured to sign before seeing a detailed design, clear total price and cancellation rights.

These are reasons to ask for evidence or a second quote, not proof that any particular provider is unsuitable.

### 6. Next useful step

Use the government suitability checker first. Then seek a detailed design and quotation from an appropriately qualified, **MCS-certified** installer. Compare the proposed design, full cost, likely running-cost assumptions, service support and applicable government help.
