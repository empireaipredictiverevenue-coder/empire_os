# Is your home right for solar panels? What to know before getting quotes

If you're considering solar panels, the first question isn't which installer has the biggest discount. It's whether your roof, electricity habits and budget support a good installation in the first place.

Solar photovoltaic (PV) panels make electricity from daylight. They don't directly heat water like solar thermal collectors. Solar PV can work in the UK even on cloudy days, but generation varies greatly by season, roof orientation and shade. A sensible decision starts with **what your home can realistically generate, how much of that electricity you can use and what each full quotation actually includes**.

## Check the roof before choosing a system size

A competent survey should look at the roof's condition, usable dimensions, orientation, pitch, shading and loading. Ask what happens if roof repairs would be required before the panels are installed. If your roof is close to needing replacement, removing and reinstalling a PV system later could add costs.

A south-facing, unshaded roof is a strong starting point, but an east- or west-facing roof can also work. A north-facing roof typically produces less and needs particularly careful assessment. Don't reject a property based on compass direction alone: ask the installer for **a property-specific annual generation estimate** that explains its assumptions.

Shade can come from chimneys, dormers, trees and nearby buildings. It changes across the day and year. Ask the surveyor whether their calculation accounts for winter shadows, future tree growth and any proposed panel-level power optimisers. Don't accept an optimiser as an automatic improvement on a roof that has no meaningful shading problem.

An Energy Saving Trust example of a 4.5 kWp system uses roughly 20–30 m² of roof area; that is an illustration, not a roof-space rule for every product or house.

**Independent starting point:** [Energy Saving Trust: Solar panels](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/solar-panels/).

## Ask how much electricity your household will actually use

A solar system's rated capacity is measured in **kWp**, while the electricity it generates and the electricity you use are measured in **kWh**. These are different numbers.

What matters financially is not just the forecast kWh generated over a year, but when that electricity is produced relative to your household demand. If you're home during the day, you may use more solar electricity immediately. If most of your use is after sunset, you may export more or investigate time-shifting appliances.

Start with your electricity bills and, if available, suitable meter data. Tell the installer if you use or expect to use an electric vehicle, heat pump or other major appliance. Ask them to show the **estimated on-site use** and **estimated export** separately. Both should be supported by a stated generation forecast.

Consider using Energy Saving Trust's free [solar panel calculator](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/tool/solar-energy-calculator/) before asking for final quotations. Its outputs are estimates—not warranties of individual savings.

## Understand export payments before accepting a sales forecast

If you export spare electricity, you may be eligible for payments under the **Smart Export Guarantee (SEG)** in Great Britain. It is **not an automatic payment simply because panels are installed**.

You generally need eligible certified equipment and installation, a meter capable of recording exported electricity and a contract with an export-tariff provider. Suppliers set their own rates and terms, and the rate isn't necessarily the same as what you pay to import electricity.

Ask any installer quoting SEG income to identify the exact export tariff assumed, eligibility requirements and whether the quote assumes you have already signed up. You can compare SEG providers independently; you don't necessarily have to use the company that sells you imported electricity.

**Independent sources:** [Ofgem: Smart Export Guarantee](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/smart-export-guarantee-seg) and [Energy Saving Trust: SEG explained](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/smart-export-guarantee).

## Is adding a battery worth it?

A battery can store spare solar generation for later. But that doesn't mean every homeowner needs one. Whether it pays back depends on the installed cost, usable capacity, your evening electricity use, tariff structure, battery lifetime, expected export income and any additional electrical work.

Ask installers to quote **panels without a battery** and **panels with a battery** separately. Compare the extra battery cost against realistic changes in self-consumption and export revenue. A battery may also charge from cheaper grid electricity under suitable tariffs, which changes the analysis.

Don't assume battery storage guarantees that your lights will keep working in a power cut: backup power often needs a specifically designed system and suitable isolation equipment. Ask what circuits, if any, the proposed equipment can supply when the grid is down.

**Independent source:** [Energy Saving Trust: Battery storage](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/solar-panel-battery-storage/).

## Check installer credentials, DNO and permissions

Look for an installer whose relevant work is certified by **MCS or an officially recognised equivalent** and verify their current status using the scheme's own register. If you intend to use the SEG, check the specific certification requirements before signing.

Your installer should explain the electrical connection, metering and the **Distribution Network Operator (DNO)** notification or permission process that applies to your proposed system. Clarify who is responsible and when an application needs approval before work starts.

Planning permission is not generally required for many domestic roof installations in England, but there are exceptions. Listed buildings, designated areas, flats, leasehold homes and unusual mounting arrangements deserve specific checks with the planning authority and any landlord or freeholder. England's solar permitted-development requirements were updated in **August 2026**, with transition provisions, so avoid relying on an old one-line planning rule.

**Sources:** [Citizens Advice: Finding an installer](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/energy/energy-supply/save-energy-at-home/find-an-installer-for-energy-efficiency-home-improvements/) and [Planning Portal: Solar panels on buildings](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/solar-panels/planning-permission-solar-equipment-mounted-on-a-house-or-a-block-of-flats-or-on-a-building/). The Planning Portal page focuses on England; other nations' and local requirements must be confirmed separately.

## How to compare three solar quotes fairly

An attractive total price may hide excluded work. Put each quote on the same basis:

| What to request | Quote A | Quote B | Quote C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Installer name and verified certification | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| PV capacity (kWp) and panel layout | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Expected annual generation (kWh) and calculation assumptions | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Roof/shading assessment and survey date | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Inverter make/model and warranty | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Scaffolding, mounting, roof work, wiring and VAT included | To confirm | To confirm | To confirm |
| DNO and paperwork responsibility | To confirm | To confirm | To confirm |
| Battery size and **separate** battery cost, if offered | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Assumed self-use, export tariff and annual value | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Panel, inverter, installation and workmanship warranties | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Total price, deposit, payment schedule and exclusions | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |
| Post-install support, monitoring and maintenance | To obtain | To obtain | To obtain |

Ask the installer to identify the documents you'll receive after installation, including commissioning information, warranties and certification where applicable. Get the full agreement and cancellation terms in writing before paying.

For additional independent advice on comparing installers, use [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/energy/energy-supply/save-energy-at-home/find-an-installer-for-energy-efficiency-home-improvements/). Try to get **three suitable quotations**, not just one unverified sales estimate.

## A simple way to sense-check a payback calculation

The core financial relationship is:

**Annual gross electricity value = (solar kWh used at home × the import electricity price actually avoided) + (eligible exported kWh × the actual export tariff).**

If annual generation is estimated at `G`, your solar electricity used at home is `S`, and your exported solar electricity is `E`, then the proposal should explain why `S + E` is consistent with `G`, allowing for system losses and different measurement boundaries.

For a *rough* simple payback, divide the net upfront installation cost by annual **net** value after estimated recurring costs. This ignores changing tariffs, maintenance, inverter or battery replacement, panel degradation, finance charges, future usage changes and the time value of money. It must **not** be presented as a guaranteed return.

Use numbers from a survey and your own actual tariff instead of copying a national average. If the installer won't explain the inputs or a claim sounds unusually certain, ask for a revised quote or independent second opinion.

## What to do next

Before requesting installer quotations, take these five steps:

1. Record your roof's main direction, any obvious shading and roof-condition concerns.
2. Find your annual electricity consumption in kWh from bills or your supplier's records.
3. Run the independent Energy Saving Trust solar calculator as a **starting estimate**, not a contract.
4. Ask at least three competent installers for surveys and fully itemised written quotes.
5. Compare the generation assumptions, self-use, export tariff, total work and warranties side by side.

You don't need to share your bill, postcode, phone number or email with Empire AI to use this guide. You can use independent resources and contact installers directly.
