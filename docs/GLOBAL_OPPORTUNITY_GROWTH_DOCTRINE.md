## Opportunity Foundry

Opportunity classes:
1. Market expansion — new country, region, city or niche.
2. Supply expansion — new real sources and supply partnerships.
3. Buyer expansion — new buyer classes, capacity pools, enterprise or exclusive buyers.
4. Event opportunities — storms, disasters, regulation, supply shocks and other observed events.
5. Product expansion — SaaS, API, data/intelligence, AI agents, white-label and managed services.
6. Revenue expansion — cross-sell, retention, expansion and pricing/package opportunities.
7. Organic growth — Search/AEO/GEO/citation/content opportunities.
8. Paid growth — evidence-backed advertising/distribution opportunities.
9. Partnership/distribution — agencies, affiliates, associations, marketplaces, platforms, franchise groups, publishers and agent networks.
10. Arbitrage/mismatch — observed demand/supply/capacity/pricing imbalances Empire can lawfully and operationally solve.

Lifecycle:
DISCOVER → QUALIFY → VALIDATE → EXPERIMENT → PROVE → SCALE → DEFEND → HARVEST / RETIRE

Promotion requires increasing real evidence. A model opinion is not proof.

Every proposed corridor gets a standard Market Entry Packet with, where evidence exists:
- country/jurisdiction and region/metro;
- niche/ICP and observed business density;
- buyer population and verified buyer capacity;
- acquisition sources;
- Search/AEO/GEO demand;
- competitor/coverage evidence;
- distribution/partner routes;
- price evidence;
- acquisition and fulfilment cost evidence;
- forecast revenue and forecast GP;
- actual recognized revenue and realized GP;
- language, currency and payment readiness;
- privacy/outreach/data/commercial constraints;
- product/fulfilment readiness;
- experiment design/cost;
- evidence confidence;
- current blocker;

Unknown stays UNKNOWN. Market Entry can recommend validation but cannot itself spend, publish, send outreach, sign terms, move funds or recognize revenue.

## Cheap Experiment Principle

Reduce the cost of being wrong. Before heavy scale, prefer bounded real tests:
- small real company cohort;
- small real buyer cohort;
- first-party evidence validation;
- one controlled offer;
- one landing/search/content path;
- one governed outreach path;
- real replies/conversions;
- verified economics.

A failed cheap experiment is useful if it prevents larger waste.
