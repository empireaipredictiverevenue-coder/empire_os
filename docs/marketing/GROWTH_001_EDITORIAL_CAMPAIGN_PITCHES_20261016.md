## Core idea: Stop confusing AI adoption with measurable revenue

An evidence-led B2B technical and UK SME business discussion. Two primary government datasets show that AI adoption is a real business issue, but AI use must be evaluated against the decision and outcome it is meant to improve:

- DSIT [AI Adoption Research](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-research/ai-adoption-research), based on **3,500** interviews February–May 2025, reports **16%** use at least one AI technology in that study and **77% of users** reported no revenue change at that point. This does **not** establish that AI caused no other benefit or that AI never raises revenue.
- ONS [Business Insights, 8 October 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/bulletins/businessinsightsandimpactontheukeconomy/8october2026) reports **30%** business AI adoption in September 2026 according to its different survey design. Do **not** graph 16% to 30% as a direct like-for-like time-series.
- The original Empire contribution is a clear separation of **search/click → enquiry → scoped opportunity → accepted commercial event → payment → realised value**, plus a practical comparison with *no AI* and explicit evidence and human approval requirements. Those are methods we have been designing and testing, **not published customer successes**.

**Distinct proposed angle:** *Before paying for an AI assessment, ask these six questions*.

1. Which measurable workflow are we actually trying to improve?
2. What data exists and who is allowed to share it?
3. Can a simpler non-AI fix solve the same problem?
4. What would prove the pilot improved accuracy or freed genuinely useful time?
5. What stops an experiment if it harms service quality or breaches permissions?
6. Which outputs, exclusions, timetable and decision rights appear in the written quote?
